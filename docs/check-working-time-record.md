# Check the working time record

**For each employee Well can reach, say which kind of working time record your country expects, whether a timesheet is on file for the period, and how long it has to be kept.**

## What it does

Ask whether you have to track your employee's hours and the honest answer depends on two things: the country the company is registered in, and the kind of contract the person holds. This skill reads both from what Well already holds, then names the record. A United States hire raises FLSA time records for a non exempt employee and a salary basis record for an exempt one. A German hire raises the Arbeitszeitnachweis and a holiday account. A Belgian part time hire raises the derogation register and the floating hours record, beside the work rules. An Italian hire sits in the Libro Unico del Lavoro with its attendance and overtime record. A Spanish hire raises the daily working day register and the work calendar. A French hire raises either the working time count or, on a forfait jours contract, the day count, beside the pay document retention rules and the payroll processing register.
The check is existence and kind. For each employee, the skill looks for a timesheet document on file covering the period you named and reports whether one is there. It does not report what the timesheet says. A timesheet is stored as a document with a written summary, and the total hours and the per day entries are not kept as readable fields, so no hours figure can be read back. There is no time entry, shift, attendance or absence record in Well at all, which is why this skill can tell you a record is missing but can never tell you the hours inside one are right.
Two limits shape the list of people. Employment contracts are reachable from a payslip or from a contract document you uploaded, so a hire whose first payslip has not landed yet and whose contract was never uploaded does not appear, and the answer says so rather than reporting a shorter team. And the country rules are reference prose, not a rule engine: the skill names the record and the period it is normally kept for, then points you at your payroll professional for the ruling. Well never files any of these records with anyone. The record stays with you, or it goes to the bureau that runs your payroll.

## Required data in Well

- **Country of incorporation on the workspace** (required). The country decides which record applies. When it is unset, the skill asks which country the employees work in and answers for the country you name rather than guessing one.
- **At least one payslip, or one uploaded contract** (required). The employees this skill can reach. A payslip carries the link to the employment contract, and an uploaded contract document stands on its own.
- **A period to check** (recommended). The window the timesheet check runs over. Without one the skill checks the last complete month and says which month it used.
- **Timesheet documents dropped into Well** (optional). What the on file check looks for. Their presence and the period they name are read. The hours printed inside them are not.

## FAQ

**Q: Which forms does this cover?**
A: United States: FLSA time records, the exempt classification and salary basis record, and the New York paid sick leave accrual record. Germany: Arbeitszeitnachweis and the Urlaubskonto holiday account. Belgium: the registre des dérogations for part time work, the suivi des horaires flottants for floating hours, and the règlement de travail. Italy: Libro Unico del Lavoro and the registro presenze with its overtime entries. Spain: the registro diario de jornada and the calendario laboral. France: the décompte du temps de travail, the suivi forfait jours, the conservation des documents de paie rules, and the registre des traitements RGPD for payroll. Well reads only one of these from a document you hold: a timesheet you dropped in, and then only that it exists and which period it covers. Every other one is named so you know what is due and who keeps it. None of them is generated by Well, and none of them is filed by Well.

**Q: Does Well file any of these for me?**
A: No. Well never files, submits or lodges a record with a labour inspectorate, a tax authority or a social security body. It names the record, says whether a document is on file, and states the retention period. You or your payroll professional keep it and hand it over if it is ever asked for.

**Q: Can it check whether my employee's hours are right?**
A: No. A timesheet is stored as a document with a written summary, so the total hours and the per day entries are not readable fields, and there is no time entry, shift, attendance or absence record in Well. The skill can say a timesheet for March is on file. It cannot say what March said.

**Q: Does this tell me whether I am compliant?**
A: No. The country rules are reference prose carried in the skill, not a rule engine, so the answer names the record and points you at your payroll professional for the ruling. It never declares a company compliant and never gives legal advice.

**Q: Will it tell me if an employee is misclassified as exempt?**
A: No. The exempt or non exempt status is reported exactly as it was synced onto the contract salary. Well does not decide whether a hire is classified correctly, and it does not check a salary against a federal, state or city threshold.

**Q: Why is one of my employees missing from the answer?**
A: Because Well could not reach them. Employment contracts are read from a payslip or from a contract document you uploaded, so a hire whose first payslip has not landed and whose contract was never uploaded is not in the list. The answer names that gap rather than presenting a shorter team.

**Q: Does it track holiday or sick leave balances?**
A: No. A holiday account and a paid sick leave accrual record are named as records to keep. Neither balance is computed, and neither is a forfait jours day count.

**Q: Can Well create the working time record for me?**
A: No. Well can hold a timesheet you drop in and tell you when one is missing. It does not generate a working time register on your behalf.
